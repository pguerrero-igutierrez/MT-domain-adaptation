1Motivation & Research Questions

Catalan–Basque (CA–EU) MT is severely under-resourced, especially in specialized domains. Standard multilingual models produce poor output for this pair, and in-domain parallel data is scarce.

Core question: Can synthetic domain-specific corpora built from related bilingual resources substantially improve CA–EU MT over a general baseline?
1
Can pivot-based corpus construction and back-translation create useful training data for low-resource domains?
2
Does domain-specific fine-tuning improve in-domain translation quality over the general model?
3
Is continued fine-tuning from a general checkpoint better than direct domain-specific SFT?
2Two Domains, Two Quality Criteria
Literary
Quality depends on fluency, creativity and stylistic naturalness.
Clinical
Quality depends on terminological accuracy and clinical clarity.

10Key Findings
1
Domain-specific fine-tuning is the main driver of improvement, especially in clinical ca→eu, where chrF++ rises from 11.03 to 41.51.
2
Cross-domain transfer helps but is insufficient: generalv1 out-of-domain beats zero-shot baseline but falls clearly below dedicated domain models.
3
Literary: direct SFT outperforms continued adaptation: literaryv1 > literaryv2 on all metrics — warm-starting from a general checkpoint does not help.
4
Clinical: nuanced picture between v1 and v2: clinicalv2 leads on chrF++ (41.51), BLEU (22.64) and TER (91.17), while clinicalv1 leads on COMET (74.44). Neither dominates cleanly.
5
Pipeline inconsistencies identified: v2 scripts use ES-EU auxiliary data (not CA-EU); two separate models handle translation vs fine-tuning (Latxa-Llama vs Latxa-Qwen3).
11Limitations & Future Work
Limitations: Small clinical test set (N=66); single-directional clinical training; reliance on synthetic supervision.
Corpus
Expand real parallel data and human-validated references.
Ablations
Test pivot language choices and SFT data mix ratios.
Evaluation
Add human eval and larger, balanced test sets.
Bidirectional
Extend clinical to eu→ca direction.
