1Motivation & Research Questions

Catalan–Basque (CA–EU) MT is severely under-resourced, especially in specialized domains. Standard multilingual models produce poor output for this pair, and in-domain parallel data is scarce.

Core question: Can synthetic domain-specific corpora built from related bilingual resources substantially improve CA–EU MT over a general baseline?
1
Can pivot-based corpus construction and back-translation create useful training data for low-resource domains?
2
Does domain-specific fine-tuning improve in-domain translation quality over the general model?
3
Is continued fine-tuning from a general-domain checkpoint better than direct domain-specific SFT?
2Two Domains, Two Quality Criteria
Literary
Quality depends on fluency, creativity and stylistic naturalness.
Clinical
Quality depends on terminological accuracy and clinical clarity.

10Key Findings
1
Domain-specific fine-tuning is the main driver of improvement. In particular, clinicalv1 achieves substantial gains over zero-shot Latxa-Qwen3, confirming that even moderate amounts of data (synthetic or real) are sufficient to adapt LLMs to a low-resource pair such as CA–EU.
2
Cross-domain transfer helps but is insufficient: the general domain fine-tuned model evaluated on out-of-domain data beats zero-shot baseline but falls clearly below dedicated domain models (the v1 models). This also suggests that sequential adaptation may introduce domain interference or overfitting.
3
Literary: direct SFT outperforms continued adaptation (literaryv2) and zero-shot across all metrics. The v2 model does not benefit from the general-domain checkpoint.
4
For low-resource pairs where no direct parallel data exists, synthetic data proves beneficial: Generating training pairs either via a pivot language (ES) or direct translation successfully compensates for the absolute lack of parallel corpora.
7
Clinical domains are more "learnable": Under strict token-matching, clinical SFT yields much higher overall metrics than literary SFT, reflecting the lower stylistic variance of medical terminology.
11Limitations & Future Work
Limitations: Small clinical test set (N=66); single-directional clinical training; reliance on synthetic supervision.
Corpus
High reliance on synthetic data that may introduce noise, explore SFT results on human-translated parallel data. Extend clinical to eu→ca direction.
Ablations
Test pivot language choices and SFT data sizes variation between domains.
Evaluation
Add human eval and larger, balanced test sets.
Single model
Single-architecture setup limits generalization across model families.
